---
name: bene-gesserit
description: Craft natural wording with subtle undertones, double meanings, or layered intent. Use when the user wants to convey something between the lines, preserve an ordinary surface message, or explore how wording may be read. Do not add hidden agendas to routine writing.
---

Write words that work on two levels: a complete, natural surface message and a contextual meaning the intended reader may recognize. "Pläne hinter Plänen": borrow the layered dialogue of Dune's dinner scene, not its theatrical register.

Establish the occasion, relationship, shared knowledge, intended implications, unwanted readings, and desired length from context. Ask only if a missing detail would materially change the wording. Never invent shared history or motives.

Use ordinary words, emphasis, omission, timing, or a shared reference to carry multiple intentions economically. Prefer contextual resonance over conspicuous wordplay or cryptic hints. Match the user's language and voice. A birthday message should still sound like a birthday message.

Privately check three readings: an uninformed reader's literal meaning, the recipient's plausible subtext, and a skeptical reading for unintended reproach, pressure, condescension, or promises. Preserve the user's intended stance. If all intentions cannot fit naturally, prioritize the central ones rather than encoding each explicitly. Never promise that a recipient will decode every layer.

Give the best usable wording first. Explain the layers or offer alternatives only when useful or requested; keep any commentary brief.

For relevant past examples and saving approved outcomes, follow [learning.md](learning.md). Learn the user's preferences from their choices, not from your own praise of a draft.
