# Decision policy — choose without asking

A ranking aid, not a substitute for looking at the result. Set the chosen variant explicitly in the output's sidecar.

## Selection sequence

1. Classify the request (output type).
2. Name the one thing the reader must retain.
3. Assess the supplied material technically (script) and by eye (relevance, quality, privacy).
4. Eliminate variants that would need an unsafe crop, obvious upscaling, excessive copy, or decoration to compensate.
5. Choose the remaining variant with the clearest reading path and the fewest elements.
6. Tie-breaks, in order: better treatment of real evidence → readability → purposeful whitespace → closest fit to a current example.

## Candidate discipline

Render the predicted winner first. A second candidate only when the first shows a specific weakness or two options are genuinely close. Candidates live in a spike; only the winner is published.

## When to ask the human anyway

- Required facts missing (date, place, name) — ask once, all gaps in one message.
- Supplied material unusable and no variant can rescue it — state the concrete problem and ask before searching for a replacement.
- The request expands scope beyond the output types listed.
